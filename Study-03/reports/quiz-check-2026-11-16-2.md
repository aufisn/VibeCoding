## The four items needing attention, with suggested fixes

### hist-09: Which 1919 treaty formally ended World War I between Germany and the Allied Powers?
- **Problem (guideline 1, one correct answer):** Britannica groups the 1919–1920 settlements, Versailles included, as the "Treaties of Paris" (the Peace of Paris). A player could argue "Treaty of Paris" is also right.
- **Suggested fix:** replace the option "Treaty of Paris" with **"Treaty of Brest-Litovsk"** (1918, Germany and Soviet Russia): plausible, clearly wrong. Everything else stays.
- Sources: [Britannica: Treaties of Paris (1919–1920)](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Treaties-of-Paris-1919-1920), [Britannica: What was the Treaty of Versailles?](https://www.britannica.com/question/What-was-the-Treaty-of-Versailles), [1914-1918-online: Versailles, Treaty of](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/versailles-treaty-of/)

### geo-02: In which city is the ancient Colosseum located?
- **Problem:** the explanation says the Colosseum was "completed in 80 CE under Emperor Titus". Titus opened (dedicated) it in 80 CE; the final stages were completed under his successor Domitian.
- **Suggested explanation:** "The Colosseum is an amphitheatre in the centre of Rome, opened in 80 CE under Emperor Titus."
- Sources: [History.com: Colosseum](https://www.history.com/articles/colosseum), [History Hit: The Colosseum](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/the-colosseum), [World History Encyclopedia: Colosseum](https://www.worldhistory.org/Colosseum/)

### geo-06: The Andes mountain range runs along the western side of which continent?
- **Problem (guideline 4, sources disagree):** the explanation says "about 7,000 km". Britannica gives about 8,900 km, Guinness 7,600 km, Wikipedia about 7,000 km.
- **Suggested explanation:** "The Andes run for thousands of kilometres along South America's Pacific side, from Venezuela to the southern tip of Chile and Argentina."
- Sources: [Britannica: Andes Mountains](https://britannica.com/summary/Andes-Mountains), [Guinness World Records: longest continental mountain range](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/longest-continental-mountain-range), [WorldAtlas: longest mountain ranges](https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/the-longest-mountain-ranges-in-the-world.html)

### art-04: Which composer wrote the Moonlight Sonata?
- **Problem:** the explanation says the sonata "was nicknamed the Moonlight Sonata only after his death". That's the traditional account (Britannica dates the nickname to the 1830s), but research has found the critic Ludwig Rellstab's moonlight comparison may date from Beethoven's lifetime.
- **Suggested explanation:** "Beethoven composed his Piano Sonata No. 14 in 1801; the nickname Moonlight Sonata came from the critic Ludwig Rellstab, not from Beethoven."
- Sources: [Britannica: Moonlight Sonata](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Moonlight-Sonata), [Andrew Crumey: why "Moonlight Sonata"?](https://crumey.co.uk/beethoven_5_why_moonlight_sonata.html), [Interlude: the true story behind its many myths](https://interlude.hk/beethovens-moonlight-sonata-the-true-story-behind-its-many-myths/)

## Notes on confirmed questions (no change needed)

- **sci-02:** a 2025 study suggests the red dust is mainly ferrihydrite, a water-bearing iron oxide, rather than hematite. The explanation only says "iron oxide (rust)", which remains accurate.
- **geo-04:** some historians question how seriously Sydney or Melbourne themselves were considered, but the compromise between the two rivals is the standard account (Australian Parliament fact sheet, Australian Geographic).
- **hist-07:** peak-area figures for the Mongol Empire vary (about 23–33 million km²); "largest contiguous land empire in history by area" is what Britannica says.
- **art-01:** the Louvre dates the painting 1503–1506, Britannica says Leonardo worked on it intermittently for years and it was still in his studio in 1519; "kept working on it for many years" fits.
